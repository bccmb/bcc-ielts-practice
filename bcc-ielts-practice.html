Reading Section

The greenhouse effect is a natural process that warms the Earth's surface. When the Sun's energy reaches the Earth's atmosphere, some of it is reflected back to space and the rest is absorbed and re-radiated by greenhouse gases. Greenhouse gases include water vapor, carbon dioxide, methane, nitrous oxide, ozone and some artificial chemicals such as chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs).

The absorbed energy warms the atmosphere and the surface of the Earth. This process maintains the Earth's temperature at around 33 degrees Celsius warmer than it would otherwise be, allowing life on Earth to exist. However, human activities, primarily the burning of fossil fuels and deforestation, have intensified the natural greenhouse effect, causing global warming.

1. What is the primary function of the greenhouse effect?

2. What is the main cause of the enhanced greenhouse effect?

Model Answer:

The provided chart illustrates the changes in electricity production sources in a country between 2000 and 2020. In 2000, coal was the dominant source, accounting for 50% of production, followed by gas (30%), nuclear (10%), and renewable energy (10%). By 2020, the share of renewable energy had increased to 30%, while coal decreased to 30%. Gas production declined slightly to 25%, and nuclear energy increased to 15%. This indicates a significant shift toward renewable energy sources over the two decades, reflecting the country's efforts to adopt cleaner energy solutions.
